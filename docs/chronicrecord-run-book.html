Running a chronic recording on ChronicRecord

A multi-day bout session: a Raspberry Pi Pico, plugged into the recording PC, times the cameras, the two fiber photometry LEDs and the SpikeGLX gate; campy records four FLIR cameras; SpikeGLX records the Neuropixels probe, the OneBox and the two photoreceivers. One page in the browser, campy's panel (the campy recording icon on the PC's desktop, experiment ChronicRecord session (Pico + SpikeGLX)), starts SpikeGLX, campy and the Pico, and everything lands in one folder per session on the PC's 4 TB data disk, D:. After the end the panel copies that folder to the lab's server and checks the copy byte for byte. Follow it top to bottom, from the day before to the files on disk.

Settle these before a 6-day run

  1. The photometry light levels for a long run aren't settled. On 10 November (three 15-minute gate tests with a fiber-implanted mouse), 21 µW at the fiber tip drove both photoreceivers to the OneBox's +5 V limit; 20 mA on each LED (blue 5 µW, yellow 3 µW) fitted, but only 3 mA above the current where the LEDs flicker. On 12 November a 4-hour session ran at 29 mA (blue) and 24 mA (yellow): both readings fitted (3.86 to 3.96 V), but the blue light reaching the red receiver held XA6 at +5 V in every blue flash, and the powers at the tip weren't measured (the panel saved 0 µW).   2. The cage's red camera lights reach the red receiver. With them on, XA6 sat at +5 V for a whole recording (10 November). On 12 November the patch cord's mating sleeve was wrapped and some of their light still reached both receivers, so that session too ran with the lights off and the cameras recorded a dark cage.   3. Windows Update: paused until 19 November, 5:37 PM. A PC restart ends campy and SpikeGLX, and also the Pico's session (the Pico loses its USB power). The panel wants the pause to last until a day after the session's end, so a 6-day session started now needs it longer (any start after 5:37 PM on 12 November): the panel's checks show a Pause Windows Update button (one administrator click). See This PC's settings.
  4. Never longer than 96 bouts; with a mouse, 4 hours. The longest session from the panel is the 8-hour, 96-bout rehearsal with no animal (6 November): every camera got all 278,400 frames. 1What the system is

Two devices take part, and only one of them keeps time:

  • The ChronicRecord PC (Dell Pro Max Slim FCS1250, Windows 11) runs campy, which waits for camera pulses; SpikeGLX, which records a file each time the gate goes high; and chronic_control.py, which plans the whole session, loads it onto the Pico over USB, logs what the Pico reports, and stops it. campy's panel, a page in the PC's browser opened from the campy recording desktop icon, starts SpikeGLX's run (through SpikeGLX's command server), then campy, then the Pico, all writing into one session folder on D:, the PC's second drive (a 4 TB Crucial P310 NVMe, fitted on 10 November 2026; C: is the 1 TB system drive). (The PowerShell launcher chronic6day.ps1 does the same and stays as a fallback.) You use the PC through Remote Desktop; status and stop also work over SSH.
  • Raspberry Pi Pico (on one of the PC's USB ports) makes every edge from its own crystal: gate, camera trigger, blue LED, yellow LED. If chronic_control.py or the USB link drops, the edges don't move. If the Pico loses power (a PC restart), its session ends. It is the only board on the cameras' trigger line: the rig's campy profile says so (triggerController: pico), so no config on this PC can start an Arduino on the same cameras, and the panel's checks show Trigger: the Pico. A fifth output, E (GP6), can send the OneBox a sync train that never repeats; it is off on the rig and not wired yet (see Sync pulses).
  • Fiber photometry. The blue (465 nm) and yellow (560 nm) LEDs are a photometry system's excitation light: a Doric LED driver, a Doric fluorescence minicube and one patch cord to the mouse's implanted fiber. Two New Focus 2151 photoreceivers read the emission and go to the OneBox (XA5 green, XA6 red), so SpikeGLX samples them on the same clock as every edge. See Fiber photometry.

Until 5 November 2026 a Raspberry Pi 3B ran chronic_control.py and the Pico hung off its USB. It is retired; its old logs are archived on the PC in C:\campy_data\pico\pi_archive.

One bout, to scale

Every 300 s the Pico runs one bout: gate high, 5 s wait, camera on at 60 Hz, the two LEDs 5 s into the camera (yellow 50 ms, 150 ms dark, then blue 50 ms), camera off after 10 s (240 s in bouts 1, 7, 13, … every 6th), 10 s wait, gate low. SpikeGLX adds 1 s of margin before and after the gate. The panel's page draws the same bout under Start recording, and redraws it as the bout settings change.

2Before the day

Disk space

Everything a session writes goes into one folder on the data disk, D:\data\<date_time>\: campy's videos, SpikeGLX's run (in spikeglx\) and the Pico's log. SpikeGLX is the big one: about 24 MB/s while the gate is high. The disk is a 4 TB Crucial P310 NVMe SSD, fitted on 10 November 2026; every other recording on the panel goes there too (D:\data\calibration, look, live_demo, gatetest, rehearsal). The recordings made in C:\data before then were moved to D:.

The panel checks this before the start. For the length you pick, the checks list says how much it needs: SpikeGLX's open-gate seconds plus its 1 s margins at 24 MB/s, and the videos at 0.6 MB/s a camera (an estimate on the high side), plus 2 GB kept free. For 6 days that is about 2,910 GB of the 3,840 GB free on 13 November (76%): green. Above 80% of the free space it turns yellow, a warning you can start through: once less than about 3,640 GB is free, that is after about 200 GB more of other recordings on D:. A length that doesn't fit is red, and Start recording then asks whether to start anyway, naming the shortfall. The two rates are settings in the panel config (spikeglx.write_mb_s, panel.video_mb_s).

campy's disk warning (alertDiskFreeGB: 500) watches D:, so it counts SpikeGLX's files too; on the empty data disk it comes only with less than about a day's room left.

    The setup notes also call for a 10 kΩ pull-down from each of GP2–GP5 to GND (and GP6 once it is wired), so nothing reads high while the Pico resets.

    Sync pulses (output E), for later. 10 ms pulses at random gaps of 200 to 1000 ms from a seed, through the whole session, in and between bouts, so any four in a row say exactly where in the session they are, and sync_match.py (in campy's chronicrecord folder) can tie every OneBox sample to the Pico's clock and check every other line pulse by pulse. To use it: wire GP6 to XA1 (with its pull-down), flash the Pico with chronic_timer version 3 (the rig's Pico runs version 2, which refuses a session with the sync train), set picoSync: True in the rig's campy profile, and check it with python chronic_control.py wiring --channels E %P% (five blinks on XA1). It was checked on 13 November on the camera line instead (GP3 → XA2): all 232 pulses inside SpikeGLX's files matched the plan, none missing or extra, residuals 20.6 µs at most.

Pico self-check

A 10-second camera-only burst exercises the camera pin and the Pico's own counters. The gate stays low, so SpikeGLX records nothing.

    You should seeSession started (pid …): 1 bouts, 10 s. Log: C:\campy_data\pico\logs\<date>\timestamps.txt

    PC · PowerShell

    You should see
    SYSTEM: Board finished the session at t=9.986336 s, every output low: pulses=600 late_max=0 lost=0 stalls=0 rises=600 bounces=0 self_check_us=-2

Fiber photometry: LEDs and receivers

The blue and yellow LEDs are the excitation light of a fiber photometry system, read once a bout as a "snapshot" (the pattern of the neuro-seeER reporter's paper, EGFP and FusionRed): 50 ms of yellow, 150 ms dark, 50 ms of blue. The emission comes back up the same fiber to two photoreceivers, which the OneBox samples every 33 µs, on the same clock as the gate, the camera pulses and the LED lines.

  1. The receivers on DC Low (2×10¹⁰ V/A, DC to 750 Hz), both of them. On their AC settings a flash's steady level is filtered out: each flash shows as a jump that sags back within the flash. Fresh 9 V batteries in both: a flat battery reads a steady 0.2 V or so that doesn't move when its LED flashes (the cause of the first gate test of 10 November). How long a battery lasts hasn't been measured, so start a long run with new ones.

  2. The LED driver's two channels on TTL, following the Pico's C (blue) and D (yellow). Set each channel's current, then measure the light at the fiber tip with the power meter before the fiber is connected to the mouse. The panel asks for both numbers at every Start (below).

    10 November, mouse connected, room lights offXA5 greenXA6 red
    dark level (taken off the rows below)0.04 V0.20 V
    21 µW each at the tip (blue 115 mA, yellow 150 mA)both at the +5 V limit in their flash, every bout
    blue at 20 mA (5 µW)2.87 V4.07 V (blue light reaching the red receiver)
    yellow at 20 mA (3 µW)0.01 V3.27 V

    At 20 mA the three bouts' readings were within 0.25% (green) and 0.4% (red) of each other. But both LEDs flicker at 17 mA and are off at 16 mA, so 20 mA is close to the edge, and XA6 in the blue flash at 4.1 V is close to the limit. The suggestion for long runs is a neutral density filter of about OD 0.5 in each LED's cord, between the LED and the cube, which would put these powers at roughly 65 to 75 mA (worked out between two measured points per LED, not tried). Another mouse's implant may want other powers: check with the gate test.

    On 12 November, four hours (49 bouts) at 29 mA blue and 24 mA yellow, sleeve wrapped, lights off: green_V 3.86 to 3.90 V and red_V 3.86 to 3.96 V, never clipped, dark levels near 0 V; XA6 sat at +5 V through every blue flash, so the blue light's bleed into the red receiver couldn't be measured. Red scattered more between bouts than green (0.48% against 0.16%) and carried a 727 Hz ripple, about 26 mV rms, only while the yellow LED was on, most likely from its driver at 24 mA (the reading's 16.7 ms window averages it out). Measure the powers at the tip before every session: skipped, the panel saves 0 µW.

  3. Keep the room's red camera lights out of the fiber. With them on, XA6 sat at +5 V for a whole recording, dark included: red light passes through tissue, and the red receiver takes 580–680 nm. The tests with the mouse ran with the lights off. Before a long run, make the mouse's end light-tight (black heat-shrink over the ferrule's mating sleeve was not enough on 12 November: a cover over the headcap too, or infrared camera lights), and check XA6's dark level with the lights on.

  4. Run the panel's 15-minute gate test with the mouse connected and read it with photometry_snapshots.py (see Reading the photometry): no reading at 5 V (its note column says so), and each LED's reading steady from bout to bout.

SpikeGLX

The panel (and the launcher) starts and stops SpikeGLX's run itself, through SpikeGLX's command server. It also opens SpikeGLX if it isn't open (with SpikeGLX's own saved settings: probe, trigger, command server) and does the Configure dialog's Detect and Verify itself (you'll see that dialog open and close by itself: don't click in it). If SpikeGLX is already open, leave it idle (no run). The command server only needs turning on once: Options → Command Server Settings → Enable (address 127.0.0.1, port 4142; it listens on the PC only). It checks the trigger is TTL before it starts anything; these are the settings it found on the rig:

The panel now enables it, once the OneBox's samples are flowing (SpikeGLX says it is running about 6 s before they are), and then proves it with a gate blink.

Gate-blink control

The panel does this itself, right after it starts SpikeGLX's run: the gate goes high once for 0.5 s, and SpikeGLX must open a file for it (file t0 of the run, 2.5 s long with its margins), or the panel stops before campy starts. To do it by hand (with SpikeGLX running and recording enabled):

  1. PC · Anaconda Prompt
    python chronic_control.py wiring --channels A %P%
    You should seeWiring check started (pid …): …, until 'stop'. Log: …/wiring.txt, and within a second or two, a new SpikeGLX file (a few seconds long: the 0.5 s blink plus its 1 s margins).

  2. Stop it after the first blink. It would otherwise keep blinking, and each blink opens a file.

    PC · Anaconda Prompt
    python chronic_control.py stop %P%
    You should seeSession stopped: wiring check stopped after 1 rounds; status then says Wiring check: done, … 1 rounds. No new file in SpikeGLX? Recording isn't enabled or the trigger is wrong: fix that before going on.

3Starting a recording

The order matters, and the panel keeps it. SpikeGLX first, then campy, then the Pico, once every camera is grabbing: a camera that isn't grabbing when the first pulse comes misses frames the others have. Use the panel from the PC's own screen or through Remote Desktop (it answers only on the PC itself), and leave its black window open for the whole session: it is the panel, and it holds the rig lock.

  1. Open the panel: double-click campy recording on the PC's desktop (the rig's one icon, for every kind of recording), and pick ChronicRecord session (Pico + SpikeGLX) in the Experiment box at the top of the page. The page opens on the experiment picked last on this PC, which on 12 November was this one.

    You should seea black window filling with lines (leave it open; Ctrl-C in it does nothing while it holds the rig), and the panel's page in the browser, with the software's version at the top right (v2.8.0 +… · dev · 7952d90…). If the browser doesn't open, the black window's first lines give the address (http://127.0.0.1:8765/?t=…): copy it whole into the browser. A+ at the top left makes the page's text larger. SpikeGLX can be closed or open and idle: the panel opens it at Start if needed.

  2. Before the start: pick the session length (6 days, a 15-minute gate test of 3 bouts, an hour, or an 8-hour overnight rehearsal of 96 bouts), fill in the experiment name, animal IDs and notes, check the copy folder, and read the checks.

    You should seethe plan for that length (for 6 days: 1,728 bouts, 5,011,200 camera pulses) and, under Bout settings, the bout's settings with a line saying what one bout does (yellow LED at 9.999 s for 50 ms (frames 300-302), then 150 ms dark, then blue for 50 ms (frames 312-314)) and a drawing of it. Leave them as the config has them: a changed setting is outlined, and Back to the config's undoes every change. Then a list of checks, each ok (green), warning (yellow) or stops the start (red): Trigger: the Pico, which answers and is idle; SpikeGLX answers (or is closed), is idle, and its trigger is TTL; Windows Update is paused until a day after the session (if not, a Pause Windows Update button: click Yes in Windows' prompt); USB selective suspend is off; and the disk (6 days: about 2,910 GB of D:'s free space, green below 80%).     Copy each recording to: picking 6 days or the gate test fills this box with the lab's server folder set in the rig's config; the hour and the rehearsal put back the folder last used for them (empty for none). Change it if this recording should go elsewhere. Delete each recording from this computer once its copy is checked is off unless you tick it: tick it only if the server folder is where the session is kept for good (see Copies). If the page can't reach the folder at Start, it says so and doesn't start.

  3. Press Start recording, and confirm the LED power. The boxes hold the last values confirmed, with when (yellow if from an earlier day). If you changed a current, measure that LED again and change its power too: Confirm and start saves both boxes as they are, into the recording's session.json. The recording doesn't start without them.

    You should see, in order: SpikeGLX: if it was closed, it opens and its Configure dialog opens and closes by itself (don't click in it), then its run starts into the session's spikeglx\ folder with recording on; Gate check: the gate blinks once and SpikeGLX opens a file for it (file t0), or nothing else starts and the page says what to check; Cameras: campy starts and one campy-live-view window shows the 4 cameras, dark until the Pico pulses (the cameras take a frame only on a pulse; closing that window doesn't touch the recording). The page's camera cards say No picture yet: the camera waits for the Pico's pulses. The Pico card counts Cameras getting ready: n of 4, then turns green: All 4 cameras are ready. Press Start the Pico., with a short chime, and the browser tab's title changes to Cameras ready · campy panel. No need to watch the black window.

  4. Start the Pico: once the page says all 4 cameras are ready, press Start the Pico (it asks first), or let it start at the time you gave. If a camera isn't ready, the button says so: stop, and start again.

    You should seethe Pico card showing bout 1 of 1,728, the camera pulses so far and when the session ends; the SpikeGLX card showing its run, a file open while the gate is high, and the number of files; each camera's picture and frame rate. Between bouts each camera shows a grey PAUSED with how long (in the page and in campy-live-view), not a red NO FRAMES, and when the next bout starts no camera turns red with LOST. Once a bout's gate has fallen, the Pico card says between bouts (1728 in all). Seen on the rig on 6 November.

  5. Leave Remote Desktop. Disconnect (close the window); don't sign out, which would end the panel, campy and the Pico's program. Closing the browser tab is fine: the page can be opened again from the desktop icon at any time.

Without the panel (a fallback): the PowerShell launcher does the same steps from a PowerShell window in Remote Desktop: powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File C:\campy_data\launchers\chronic6day.ps1 (add -PicoStartAt "10:00 AM" to start the Pico at a time). 5Stopping and after

At the planned end

Nothing to do. campy stops by itself after its 5,011,200th frame per camera; the Pico's session ends 10 s later, when bout 1,728's gate falls. The panel waits for that, then stops SpikeGLX's run (closing its files).

You should seethe page show the session as ended, with the Pico's last counts (pulses=5011200 … stalls=0 bounces=0) and SpikeGLX's run stopped. If campy ended early on its own (a camera lost, say), the Pico and SpikeGLX are left running so the LED schedule goes on and is recorded: the page lists them under Still running, with Stop the Pico, then Stop SpikeGLX's run, for when you decide. Close the panel with Close the panel at the top of the page once nothing records (and the copy is verified). Closing only the browser tab leaves the panel running, still holding the rig: on 12 November that kept a queued job waiting until the next morning.

Stopping early

Press Stop on the page. It asks first, and offers two ways:

  • Stop after this bout: the Pico finishes the bout it is in, so its gate falls on time and SpikeGLX's file closes as planned; then campy and SpikeGLX stop.
  • Stop now: the Pico stops at once (every output low), then campy (each camera closes its video), then SpikeGLX's run.

From another computer over SSH, the Pico alone can be stopped with python chronic_control.py stop %P% (after this bout) or stop --now (see Checking on the Pico); campy then keeps waiting for frames until the panel's Stop, or its stop file.

Copies

With a folder in Copy each recording to, the panel copies the session's folder there after the post steps, in the background, then reads every file back from both places and checks they are the same, byte for byte. The page lists it under Copies (copying, checking, verified, or failed with the reason) and, while it runs, a Copying in the background strip at the top with the percent done, the time left and when it should be done. A new recording can start meanwhile; leave the PC on and the panel open until the copy is verified. Close the panel waits for the copies, then closes by itself.

  • How long: a 7.6 GB gate test (mostly one 5.5 GB SpikeGLX file) copied to a network share in about 7 minutes (17 MB/s), and the check takes about as long again. At that rate a 6-day session's 2.9 TB would take about two days to copy and two more to check: an estimate, nothing that size has been copied.
  • Deleting here: only with the delete box ticked, and only once every file is the same in the copy. A session that didn't stop cleanly, a file that differs, or a file a program still has open keeps it here, and the page says why (an alert too). Nothing is ever deleted in the copy.
  • By hand: each recording's logs\post.log gives the command that copies it, for a copy that was cut short.

Check the counts

  • Pico: pulses=5011200 in the last Board finished line of session.out, with stalls=0 bounces=0.
  • campy: 5,011,200 frames on every camera in the end message and each metadata.csv, none dropped. In the 1-hour rehearsal: 34,800 = 34,800 on all four; in the 8-hour one, 278,400 = 278,400 on all four, "0 dropped by campy, 0 missed by the camera" in post.log.
  • SpikeGLX: 1,729 files: the gate check's t0 (2.5 s, no camera pulses), then one per bout, each with 600 or 14,400 camera pulses on XA2, the first 5.000 s after the gate.

Lining video up with SpikeGLX

SpikeGLX records every edge, so its clock is the reference. Frame k of a bout is camera pulse k on XA2 in that bout's file. Skip t0 (the gate check) and walk the bouts in order: bout 1 (t1) holds frames 1–14,400, bout 2 the next 600, and so on (bouts 1, 7, 13, … are long). Each camera's aligned.csv gives every frame's own Pico pulse, so a stray frame shifts nothing. With the panel's pattern, yellow lights frames 300–302 of each bout and blue 312–314 (counted from 0 at the bout's first pulse), a check visible in the video.

The clocks, measured over the 8-hour rehearsal: counted at its nominal 30,303 samples/s, the OneBox's clock gains 3.21 parts per million on the Pico's (91.6 ms over the 7.9 hours from bout 1 to bout 96, 0.28 s a day), steadily: no bout was more than 72 µs off a straight line. The Pico and the PC's clock agree to 0.05 ppm, so the 3.2 ppm is the OneBox's sample rate, not the Pico (the 1-hour rehearsal gave 3.2 ppm too). It doesn't matter for lining up, because each pulse's time comes from SpikeGLX itself; SpikeGLX's own calibrated sample rate, if set, takes it out. With the sync output wired (see Sync pulses), python sync_match.py <session folder>, run on a copy, fits the OneBox's samples to the Pico's clock through every sync pulse and lists any camera, gate or LED edge the Pico didn't plan.

Reading the photometry

photometry_snapshots.py (in campy's chronicrecord folder; needs numpy only) reads a session's OneBox files and writes photometry_snapshots.csv, one row per bout. Run it on a copy of the session (the server's), not on the rig while it records:

Any computer with the copy · Python
python photometry_snapshots.py <session folder> --campy <session folder>
You should seeper bout: each flash's time on the OneBox's clock, its width, the frames it lit and its lead before its first frame's pulse; green_V (XA5 in the blue flash) and red_V (XA6 in the yellow one), each the mean over the flash's last 16.7 ms (one mains cycle) less the dark before the first flash; the bleed between channels; how fast each receiver rose; and a note for any sample at the OneBox's ±5 V limit. It ends with the order, frames, widths, gap and leads as ranges over all bouts, which should be the same in every bout. Flashes under 18.7 ms are read over less than a mains cycle, and its note says so.

7What hasn't been tested yet

  • More than 96 bouts. The longest Pico session on the rig was the 8-hour, 96-bout rehearsal from the panel (6 November, 1:38 to 9:34 AM).
  • A full 6 days, or any session over 8 hours: disk, Windows updates or sleep over days.
  • Surviving a PC restart. On USB power the Pico loses power and its session ends (measured 5 November). A separate 5 V supply was considered and isn't planned (6 November): keep Windows Update paused and don't restart the PC during a session.
  • A mouse for longer than 4 hours, or with the camera lights on. With a fiber-implanted mouse: three 15-minute gate tests (10 November) and one 4-hour session (12 November, 49 bouts), all with the room lights off and a Neuropixels 2.0 probe plugged in but not implanted. Video sizes at 60 fps with mice are still estimated from 30 fps recordings; the panel's disk check uses 0.6 MB/s a camera until a real session gives a measured rate.
  • Photometry over days: the mouse end made light-tight with the camera lights on, the LEDs behind a neutral density filter, how long the receivers' 9 V batteries last, and the cords from the LEDs to the cube.
  • The sync output on the rig: GP6 wired to XA1, and whether SpikeGLX's digital copy of XA1 (XD0 bit 1) shows it. Checked only on the camera line (13 November).
  • Copying a session of terabytes to the server, and the check after it: only gate tests of a few GB have been copied.
  • The chronic6day.ps1 launcher in a real session, and the one-hour first-frame wait on the rig (covered by campy's tests, not yet seen live). Its sister gatetest_pc.ps1 ran the 3-bout gate test from the PC on 5 November, including SpikeGLX opened by the launcher and an early stop with Ctrl-C. Both now default to D:\data, and keep chronic_control.py's own LED pattern (blue 30 ms, then yellow), not the panel's.
  • A 6-day session on D:. The data disk has held gate tests and a calibration since 10 November, nothing longer.
  • SpikeGLX's calibrated sample rate for the OneBox: the 8-hour rehearsal shows the OneBox runs 3.2 ppm off its nominal 30,303 samples/s against both the Pico and the PC (see Lining video up with SpikeGLX). It doesn't affect lining up the data.
