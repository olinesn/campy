# Watching it live (campy-live-view)

`campy-live-view` shows the cameras and campy-live's tracking while campy records. Setting up the live pipeline is in [Live tracking](live-tracking.md).


`campy-live-view` shows the cameras' frames as campy records them, with campy-live's results drawn on them:
- **3D**: each animal's 3D pose, projected into the camera through the calibration: filled dots and thick lines, one colour per identity. Its label gives the identity (`#0`, `#1`, ...) and how far, in that camera, the projection is from the 2D it was made from (px, mean over the nodes).
- **2D**: what the model found in that camera: open circles and thin lines, in the colour of the identity campy-live matched them to, or white when it matched them to none.
- A line from each detection to its identity's 3D.

When campy-live matched an animal right, its open circles sit on its dots. A long line, or a mouse with open circles of one colour and dots of another, is a wrong match. A panel lists each identity's cameras and reprojection error, and how long after its frames each result was ready and shown.

Beside the cameras, a **3D view** shows the same result in 3D: each animal's skeleton in its identity's colour (the same colours as on the cameras), the cameras as small pyramids where the calibration puts them, and a floor grid (grid squares in the calibration's units, mm). It starts looking at the cage from the cameras' side, from far enough that the mice and every camera are in view; drag in it to turn it, right-drag to move it, use the wheel to zoom, and double-click to put it back.

On the 3D view's floor, each mouse leaves a **trail** in its colour: where it walked over the last 60 seconds (`--trail-seconds`), fading as it gets older (`t` turns the trails off and on). Press `o` for the **occupancy** map (or start with `--occupancy`): the floor's squares light up the more time the mice spent over them, since campy-live started, or over the last few minutes with `--heat-minutes 10`. Both count only mice seen well by at least two cameras, so a false detection outside the cage does not paint the map. The calibration does not say where the floor is (its world is usually the first camera's), so the view works it out: up starts as away from where the cameras look, then becomes the plane the mice walk on, with the floor under their lowest well-placed body points; a few seconds of mice moving about are enough. If it gets up wrong, give it with `--up x,y,z` (a direction in the calibration's world).

**Problems at a glance.** The view also watches the rig and the tracking, so that you do not have to read numbers to know something is wrong:
- **Each camera's tile** shows its frame rate in green at its top right. When something is wrong with that camera, it says what in a red label instead, and the tile gets a red frame: the camera is behind the others or has stopped (`12.0 FPS`, `NO FRAMES`); it lost frames (`LOST 2`: its own timestamps skipped them), frame numbers are missing from its feed (`GAP 1`), or frames reached the computer a frame late (`LATE 1`), in the last 10 seconds; or it is a frame off the other cameras (`1 FRAME AHEAD`), when its frames with a given number were taken a trigger pulse before or after the others'. That last one happened on 2026-10-20: a camera that kept grabbing after a double Ctrl+C started the next recording one frame ahead (31 ms off), and campy-live called every set "misaligned". If you see it, stop the recording and start it again. The view reads all this from the live feed itself (each frame's number and times, not the images), about 20 times a second.
- **The panel** starts with whatever is wrong, in red, in plain sentences. Then comes a strip per mouse identity showing its last 10 seconds: the bar is full height when that mouse was triangulated from every camera and shorter from fewer, green when its 3D fits the cameras' 2D within 10 px, yellow within `--bad-px` (20 px by default), red above that, and gray when it had no 3D at all. It warns when fewer mice than campy-live's `--animals` have good 3D, or when one mouse's error is far above the others'. Below that, it says how long the recording has been going, each camera's frame rate, and how far apart the cameras' frame times are.
- **A mouse whose 3D is badly off** (reprojection error above `--bad-px`) is drawn dimmed and dashed, on the cameras and in the 3D view, with its label in red (`#2  bad 3D: 34 px`), so that a 3D point made from a false detection (on equipment outside the cage, say) no longer looks as trustworthy as a good one. campy-live leaves out any node whose cameras disagree by more than 50 px (pose3d's `reproj_threshold`), so the error shown stays under about 50 px. The view goes by the mouse's error over the last half second (its median), not by each frame's alone, so the dimming stays put instead of blinking: on the recordings of 2026-10-20 a mouse's error crossed 20 px 100 to 180 times a minute, often for a single frame, while its half-second median crosses it 13 to 26 times a minute, and the mice are dimmed for as much of the time as before (within 3 percentage points). A mouse that goes badly off is dimmed about a quarter of a second later. `--bad-px 0` turns this off.

It runs in campy-live's environment, with OpenCV (`pip install opencv-python`) and, for its Qt window, PyQt5 (`pip install PyQt5`), on the computer that records, as a program of its own at a lower priority. Start it before or after the others; it stays open from one recording to the next:
```
campy-live-view                   # every camera, in a grid
campy-live-view --camera 0        # camera 0 alone
```
The window is Qt's (PyQt5, as campy's preview windows) when PyQt5 is installed in campy-live's environment, with real checkboxes and buttons in a panel beside the view, and the labels on the pictures (camera names, frame numbers, the mice's labels) written in the computer's own fonts, sharp at any window size; otherwise it is OpenCV's, with the panel drawn under the cameras (`--gui qt` or `--gui opencv` to choose). Both work the same way:
- **Each picture's header** names the camera, then its frame number with thousands separators and the frame's time into the recording ("frame 9,149  0:05:04.93", the same clock as the panel's "Recording" line), and how many mice it found.
- **What is drawn**: the predictions (2D), the reprojections (3D), the lines, the 3D view and its trails and occupancy, on or off with the panel's checkboxes or the keys `p` (or `d`) and `r` (the predictions and the reprojections, as in LUC3D), `l`, `\`, `t` and `o` (`--no-3d` starts with the 3D view off). With the trails on, each camera's picture has trails too, as LUC3D's node trails: each point's path over the last quarter second, older thinner, fainter and darker, in the mouse's colour (the Qt panel's "Camera trails" picks the length, ¼ to 2 s, and the shade; `--camera-trail-seconds`, `--trail-shade`).
- **One camera or all of them**, with LUC3D's keys: `v` shows one camera alone (the one you last clicked, outlined in the grid), with the 3D view beside it unless you closed it with `\`; `g` goes back to the grid; the arrow keys go to the previous or next camera. Double-clicking a camera shows it alone, and double-clicking again goes back to the grid. 1-9 also show that camera alone, and `a` (as well as `g`) the grid.
- **Zoom into a camera**, also as in LUC3D: turn the mouse wheel over a camera's picture to zoom in or out around the mouse pointer, and drag to move around in it (a click without dragging still picks the camera). `+` (or `=`) and `-` zoom every camera shown, `0` shows the whole frames again, and Shift+0 shows them at their own resolution (one camera pixel per screen pixel; press it again to go back). The drawing follows the zoom exactly. Each camera keeps its zoom when you switch between the grid and one camera. (`0` used to show the grid; use `g` or `a` for that now.)
- **Other keys**: `z` fits the window to the view (no black margins), space pauses, `s` saves the view as a PNG, `q` quits. The window comes to the front when it opens and when a recording starts. It opens at `--size` (1800x960) or, on a smaller screen, as large as fits beside the taskbar; `z` keeps it on the screen too (the view smaller, in its proportions). Once a recording ends, the panel no longer shows its tracking strip. Resize it as you like: the view is drawn at the window's own size, one screen pixel per pixel, the frames in their proportions (never larger than they are), with black around them. Without a desktop (over SSH), `campy-live-view --no-window --png <folder>` saves every 30th view instead, and ends with the recording. `python -m pydoc campy.liveview` says more.
- **Bookmarks**: press `m` (or the panel's **Bookmark** button) to mark a moment you want to find again later, in the videos or in LUC3D. Shift+1 to Shift+9 (or the panel's 1-9 buttons) mark it as event 1 to 9, for kinds of event you pick yourself (say 1 for a fight, 2 for rearing). In the Qt window you can type a short note in the box above the button first; it goes with the next bookmark (Enter in the box also makes one; Esc leaves the box). A bookmark is saved to disk the moment you make it, so a crash loses none. The view flashes a yellow outline, and the panel lists the last few (OpenCV's window: the last one). Paused, a bookmark marks the frame on the screen.

Bookmarks go into `bookmarks.csv` in the recording's folder, beside `Camera0`, `Camera1`, ...: each camera's live feed says where that camera records, and the view reads it there. With a `campy-acquire` from before this version, whose feed does not say, they go to `--bookmarks` (default `campy-live-view_bookmarks.csv` in the folder the view was started from). A view started again adds to the same file. One line per bookmark:

| Column | What it is |
|---|---|
| `number` | 1, 2, 3, ... in the file |
| `time` | the computer's clock, local time, to the millisecond |
| `host_ns` | the same moment on the clock of campy's `hosttimes.npy` (campy's host clock, `campy/clock.py`) |
| `event` | `mark`, or 1 to 9 |
| `note` | the note, or empty |
| `frame` | the frame number on the screen: campy's frame number, as in each camera's `frametimes.npy` (empty if no frames were shown yet) |
| `frame_0`, `host_ns_0`, `frame_1`, ... | each camera's newest frame when the key was pressed, and when it reached the computer (as in `hosttimes.npy`); in camera order (`Camera0` is 0). Usually a frame or two after `frame`: the view shows each result about 40 ms after its frames |
| `recording` | the recording's folder, or empty |

campy numbers frames from 1, and a camera's video holds the frames listed in its `frametimes.npy`, in order. So bookmark `frame` *n* is video frame *n* − 1 (counting from 0) when the camera dropped no frame, and in general:
```
import csv, numpy as np
numbers = np.load("Camera0/frametimes.npy")[0]            # the frame numbers in Camera0's video
for mark in csv.DictReader(open("bookmarks.csv")):
    if mark["frame"]:
        print(mark["number"], mark["event"], mark["note"], np.searchsorted(numbers, int(mark["frame"])))   # its video frame, from 0
```

Each result is drawn on the frames it was made from, never on newer ones. campy-live puts each set's row in shared memory as it logs it (`campy-live-results`, laid out in `campy/liveresults.py`; `--results-feed off` stops that), and the view reads that row's frame number back from the live feed, which holds each camera's newest 8 frames (0.27 s at 30 fps). A result whose frames are gone by then is counted and left out.

It costs campy-live almost nothing. On the Dell Pro Max Slim FCS1250 (3 cameras of 1280x1024 at 30 fps, 3 mice, a recording replayed into the feed, 60 s twice each way), campy-live's inference took 27.44 and 27.45 ms (p50) alone and 27.54 and 27.53 ms with the view running, and it skipped no set either way. Drawing one view of the 3 cameras takes the view 7-8 ms of one core. Results reach it 30 ms after their frames (p50; p99 33), and each is drawn by 38 ms (p99 41).

The 3D view adds about 1.5 ms per result (p50; p99 3.5), and campy-live does not notice it. With the 3D view on (same replay, 60 s runs), campy-live's inference took 27.65, 27.60, 27.54, 27.52 and 27.61 ms (p50) against 27.55, 27.55 and 27.54 ms alone, it skipped no set, and its latency stayed at 31 ms (p99 32). The view drew each result in 9.0 ms (p50; 7.6 ms with `--no-3d`) and showed it 40 ms after its frames (p99 52).

Zooming, the trails, the occupancy map and Qt's labels do not slow campy-live either (same replay, 60 s runs, with the trails and the occupancy map on): campy-live's inference took 27.4 and 27.4 ms (p50) alone and 27.5, 27.5 and 27.5 ms with the view (twice without a window, once in Qt's window), it skipped no set, and its latency stayed at 31 ms (p99 32). The view drew each result in 10.5 ms (p50; p99 15-16) and showed it 42 ms after its frames. What each part costs the view, per result (3 cameras and the 3D view at 1800x960): about 7.9 ms with nothing zoomed (a little less than before: a whole frame is still scaled the fast way); zooming makes it 9.0 ms at 2x, 8.1 ms at 4x and 7.2 ms at 1:1 (only the part of the frame shown is scaled); the trails add 1.3 ms (3 mice over 60 s); the occupancy map adds nothing to most results, since it is drawn into the 3D view's background, which is drawn again about 3 ms once a second (the 3D view's p99 of 7 ms). In Qt's window, writing the labels in Qt's fonts takes Qt about 0.5 ms each time it shows a view, and the view no longer draws them (0.1-0.2 ms less).

The cameras' trails (2026-10-21, same replay, 60 s runs without a window, 3 mice): the view drew each result in 10.7 ms (p50; p99 13.9) without them, 13.8 ms (p99 17.1) with the quarter-second trails (the trails 3.0 ms of it) and 17.2 ms (p99 20.7) with 2 s trails (6.0 ms), and showed it 28.8, 31.7 and 35.3 ms after its frames; it drew every result in all three, and campy-live's inference (13.3 ms p50) and latency (16 ms) did not change. To stay that cheap, a trail is drawn in at most 8 steps of age, and one longer than 32 rows skips rows (2 s: every other frame); LUC3D, which is for going through a recording frame by frame, draws every frame.

The rig and tracking checks cost campy-live nothing either, and the view about 1.4 ms per result. Same replay, 60 s runs: campy-live's inference took 27.5, 27.6, 27.5 and 27.5 ms (p50) with the new view, 27.5 and 27.5 ms with the view before it, and 27.4, 27.4, 27.5 and 27.4 ms alone, with no set skipped and its latency at 31 ms (p99 32) in every run. The view drew each result in 10.5 ms (p99 12.5) against 9.1 ms (p99 11.3) before: about 1.1 ms for the warnings, the tiles' labels and the strip, and the rest for drawing the badly tracked mice dashed (on this recording, 2 of the 3 mice were above 20 px most of the time). Reading the cameras' frame numbers and times takes 0.4 ms, 20 times a second. On the replay it raised no warning (every camera at 30.0 frames/s), and with problems put into it (one camera a frame ahead, one losing a frame, one frame number missing) it flagged each of them on the right camera.
